# Lattafa Khamrah vs Kilian Angels Share: Is the Hype Real?

Walk into any Indian fragrance community, online or offline, and you will hear it: "Khamrah smells just like Angels Share." The [Lattafa](/house/lattafa) bestseller has built its entire reputation on this comparison. But is it true? And more importantly, does it matter?

Let us break this down honestly, because the real answer is more interesting than the hype.

## First, the Honest Disclaimer

Khamrah is **not** an official clone of Angels Share. [Lattafa](/house/lattafa) has never claimed it is. The comparison comes entirely from the fragrance community: reviewers, YouTubers, and forum members who smelled both and noticed the resemblance.

This matters because it changes what you should expect. An official clone tries to copy. Khamrah does its own thing in the same territory. Think of it less as a photocopy and more as a song in the same genre.

## What Each One Smells Like

### [Kilian Angels Share](/perfume/angelsshare-kilian)

By [Kilian](/house/kilian), the luxury niche house. The concept is a cognac barrel: the angel's share is the portion of cognac that evaporates during aging, and the perfume tries to bottle that aroma.

What you get: cognac opening with oak and cinnamon, drying down to tonka bean, praline, and vanilla. It is boozy, warm, and gourmand, but with a polished, expensive smoothness. The quality of the ingredients is obvious from the first spray. It smells rich without smelling heavy.

### [Lattafa Khamrah](/perfume/khamrah)

Cinnamon, nutmeg, and bergamot up top, then dates, praline, and tuberose in the heart, over vanilla, tonka, myrrh, and benzoin. If that sounds similar to Angels Share, that is because the structures overlap heavily: warm spice opening, sweet gourmand heart, resinous vanilla base.

The difference is in the details. Khamrah leans harder into the cinnamon and dates, giving it a spicier, slightly fruitier character. Angels Share leans into the cognac and oak, giving it a boozier, woodier feel. Side by side, they are clearly related but not identical.

## Head to Head: Scent

**The opening:** Khamrah hits harder. The cinnamon blast in the first 30 minutes is bold and attention-grabbing. Angels Share opens smoother, the cognac unfolding gradually rather than announcing itself. If you like a dramatic entrance, Khamrah wins the opening. If you like refinement, Angels Share does.

**The heart:** This is where they are closest. Both settle into a warm, sweet, spicy gourmand territory with praline and vanilla doing heavy lifting. On a blotter or on clothes, the resemblance in the middle hours is genuinely strong. This is the part of the wear that built Khamrah's reputation.

**The drydown:** Angels Share pulls ahead here. The oak, tonka, and vanilla meld into something seamless and expensive-smelling that lasts. Khamrah's drydown is pleasant but simpler, more linear vanilla-sweetness. The gap in ingredient quality shows most in the final hours.

**Verdict on scent:** They share maybe 75 to 80 percent of their DNA. Close enough that most people would call them similar. Different enough that owning both is not redundant.

## Head to Head: Performance

Here is where it gets interesting, because conventional wisdom says the expensive niche fragrance should outperform. It does not, quite.

**Longevity:** Khamrah is a beast. On skin, it easily goes 8 to 10 hours, and on clothes it can last days. Angels Share lasts well too, around 8 hours, but it is more of a close-to-skin luxury wear in the later hours while Khamrah keeps projecting.

**Projection:** Khamrah projects harder, especially in the first few hours. Two sprays fill a room. Angels Share is more restrained, which suits its luxury positioning but means you get less presence per spray.

**Verdict on performance:** Khamrah wins on raw power. Angels Share wins on refinement of that power. For Indian winters and wedding season, Khamrah's projection is arguably more useful.

## Head to Head: Value

This is the part that made Khamrah famous, so let us be direct.

Angels Share is priced as ultra-luxury niche. In India, you are looking at five figures for a bottle, assuming you can find it at a counter or a trusted importer. It is a special purchase, not a casual one.

Khamrah sits at a mass-market price point. It costs a small fraction of Angels Share, the kind of price where you can blind-buy it without anxiety. Check the [live price on our Khamrah page](/perfume/khamrah) to see current Indian seller prices.

**Verdict on value:** There is no contest. Khamrah delivers the vast majority of the experience at a tiny fraction of the cost. This is the entire reason the comparison exists.

## So, Is the Hype Real?

Yes, but with nuance.

The hype says "Khamrah smells exactly like Angels Share." That is an exaggeration. It smells *like* Angels Share in the way a good cover song sounds like the original: recognisable, enjoyable, but its own thing.

The smarter framing: Khamrah is an excellent boozy-spicy gourmand that happens to share territory with one of niche perfumery's most beloved fragrances. At its price, it does not need to be identical to be worth it. It needs to be good, and it is.

**Buy Khamrah if:** you want the warm, boozy, cinnamon-dates experience for winter and festive wear without spending luxury money. This is one of the easiest recommendations in Indian fragrance.

**Buy Angels Share if:** you have the budget, you appreciate ingredient quality and blending refinement, and you want the polished original. It is genuinely beautiful.

**Buy both if:** you are serious about gourmands. They are different enough to justify coexisting, Khamrah for loud winter nights, Angels Share for refined evenings.

## The Khamrah Family: Flankers Worth Knowing

Khamrah's success spawned a whole line, and two flankers deserve mention:

**[Khamrah Qahwa](/perfume/khamrah-qahwa)** takes the original's DNA and adds a coffee note, pushing it further into gourmand territory. If you find regular Khamrah slightly too spicy, Qahwa's coffee smooths it out. It is arguably even more winter-appropriate than the original.

**Khamrah Dukhan** (smoke in Arabic) is the smokier, more intense take. Harder to find in India than the other two, but worth knowing about if you see it. Think Khamrah with the volume turned up and a darker edge.

All three share the same excellent value proposition. If you love the original, exploring the flankers is cheap and fun.

## How to Wear Khamrah in India

A quick practical note since you are likely buying it: this is strong stuff. Two sprays maximum for most situations, three if you are outdoors in December. It is a winter and evening fragrance, not a summer office scent. Spray on clothes for maximum longevity, and give it 15 minutes for the cinnamon blast to settle before judging it.

## The Bottom Line

The hype is real but imprecise. Khamrah is not a 1:1 clone of Angels Share, and anyone telling you they are identical is overselling it. What Khamrah actually is: a genuinely good winter gourmand with a similar warm-spicy-boozy character, outrageous performance, and a price that makes it one of the best value buys in Indian fragrance. That is more than enough.
